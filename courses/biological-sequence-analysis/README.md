# Biological Sequence Analysis

Study notes for the JKU lecture *Biological Sequence Analysis* (365.060; P. Seidl, G. Klambauer, A. Regl), WS 2026/27. The notes assume no biology background. They introduce cells, DNA, RNA, proteins and the genetic code from scratch, then cover alignment and similarity, scoring schemes for DNA and proteins, log-odds scores, PAM and BLOSUM matrices, and the everyday tools: UniProt, BLAST, AlphaFold and ChimeraX. Every scoring idea comes with a small worked example.

**Status:** draft. Chapters 1–9 (lectures VO01–VO04 including the add-ons) are written.
**Missing:** chapters 10–15 (global and local alignment, MSA, phylogenetics, HMMs, AI-based sequence models) are outlines. 7 figure placeholders are still open, and there is no exam collection yet.

## Notes for editing

- File naming in the local course folder: `VOxx_<topic>` for slides (`.pdf`), recordings (`.m4a`) and transcripts (`.srt`). `videos.txt` maps each name to its Opencast ID.
- The notes combine the slides with what was said in the recordings. Side remarks and exam hints from the lecturers are included as such.
- Editorial corrections are made explicit in footnotes or the text:
  - the alignment score on the slide (−7 vs −8)
  - the match count of one example alignment
  - Dayhoff's database was PIR, not the PDB
  - NCBI does not run the PDB
  - "1 PAM ≈ 1 million years" is only a rough simplification
- PAM is written with rows = "from" (rows sum to 1). The slides use the transposed convention; the scores are the same.
- All worked numbers were re-computed by script:
  - the log-odds example
  - the three-letter PAM example, including 10/50/100/250 PAM and the identity-decay curve
  - the Markov chain powers
  - the BLOSUM toy block NF/NF/DF and the column-counting example
  - the insulin BLAST statistics (84/110 identical, 86/110 positives, 5 gaps)
- Unwritten chapters contain `\planned{...}` notes. Write them from the slides and transcripts of the corresponding lectures in the same style.
